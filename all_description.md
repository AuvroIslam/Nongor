## Crisis Tech Track

Build technology that keeps people **alive, informed, and connected** when normal infrastructure fails.

### Suitable Directions

- Internet-independent communication: mesh networks, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi Direct, LoRa, peer-to-peer relay, or store-and-forward messaging.
- Feature-phone access: SMS, USSD, IVR, or voice-first services.
- Low-resource tools: offline-first operation, small application size, low-bandwidth synchronization, and long battery life.
- Disaster response: flood or earthquake alerts, shelter and relief coordination, missing-person registries, and damage mapping.
- Civic safety: emergency broadcasts, safe check-ins, verified information distribution, and censorship-resistant publishing.
- Resilient hardware: solar or hand-crank power, rugged sensors, or community relay nodes.

These are examples, not restrictions. A project belongs in Crisis Tech when it solves a real crisis problem and remains useful under failure conditions.

### Differentiate the Idea

Many Crisis Tech teams are building offline Bluetooth or Wi-Fi Direct mesh messengers. A messenger can still be competitive, but it needs a specific advantage over similar projects:

- Who is the target user?
- Which crisis does it address?
- What does it do after a message arrives?
- What happens with no internet, limited electricity, or a button phone?
- Which field constraint does it handle better than alternatives?

Other crisis areas to consider include heat safety for outdoor workers, water and disease safety, earthquake response, fire or building collapse, blood and medicine availability, road trauma, landslides, erosion, cold waves, and ambulance routing through waterlogged roads.

## What You Can Build

There is no required technology stack. Valid formats include:

- Mobile, web, desktop, or command-line applications
- Games, bots, browser extensions, or hybrid projects
- Hardware or IoT devices

Open-source libraries, public APIs, cloud services, and pre-trained models are allowed with attribution. AI assistance is allowed, but it must be disclosed in the submission. Hardware projects may use a working prototype video and schematics when a live demo is impractical.

## Originality and Repository Rules

- All core work must be created during the 28-30 July sprint.
- Boilerplate, design systems, and open-source dependencies are allowed.
- A previous idea is allowed, but its implementation must begin during the sprint and prior work must be declared at registration.
- Plagiarised, purchased, or previously submitted projects are disqualifying.
- The repository must be public at submission time and include an OSI-approved open-source license.
- Keep meaningful commit history; do not squash the whole project into one final commit.
- Crisis Tech projects are strongly encouraged to use a permissive license such as MIT or Apache-2.0.

## Judging Criteria

| Criterion | Weight | What to demonstrate |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Impact and relevance | 25% | A real problem for real people; clear Crisis Tech fit |
| Technical execution | 20% | Working, sound, non-trivial engineering |
| Public engagement | 20% | Facebook engagement (10%) and GitHub stars (10%) |
| Innovation and originality | 15% | A genuinely new or sharper approach |
| Feasibility and resilience | 10% | Operation in low-bandwidth, low-power, low-end-device, or hostile conditions |
| Presentation and usability | 10% | A clear demo and usable interface |

For Crisis Tech, judges also assess graceful degradation: how well the project works with no internet, no electricity, or on a button phone.

## Public Engagement

Public engagement contributes 20% of the final score:

- **Facebook:** 10% for reactions and shares on one submitted public project post.
- **GitHub:** 10% for stars on the submitted public repository.

Engagement is measured at **1 August, 23:59 BST** and scored relative to the highest result in the same track.

After submitting the project, publish one public Facebook post that includes:

Do not buy reactions, shares, followers, stars, or paid boosts. Inauthentic engagement can result in zero engagement points or disqualification.
